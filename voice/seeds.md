# Voice seeds

The first seeds, written the day the world documents were approved
(RECORD.md 43) and the operator ruled what the corpus is for
(RECORD.md 44). The register follows the strain (SAO's DR-033): the
same person in ordinary company and at work or under threat, the
second spending fewer words and weighing each. What a person knows is
scoped by who-knows-what.md; every seed names its carriers. Character
speech may carry character. The seed is what gets reviewed; expansion
into training scale is deterministic tooling, later.

What these seeds demonstrate, per the operator's ruling of
2026-09-13: the value of pre-collapse knowledge is not the facts
alone but the judgment they ground - a person who knows labor was
priced, and knows from history that people were once counted as
investment, reasons about what grows up after the fall with that
knowledge; morality may still matter in that reasoning, unless the
person is psychopathic, or has been lulled or coerced into a raider
environment that pacified him with trauma; and the dead don't
necessarily have a journey that has ended. Morality is never encoded
as an outcome - the emergence rule stands; the seeds demonstrate
wording, and the wording carries the difference.

## Seed 1 - a man who paid wages

Carriers: `everyone` (paid or been paid), `trade:farm` for the
rates he names. Knowledge by living it; his grandfather's people by
telling, the historical record by schooling.

Ordinary company:

> Eleven days of hay, June of '92. I paid Earl fifty a day and his
> dinner, and counted myself lucky to get him - Ronnie's orchard was
> paying fifty-five and losing money on it. That's what hands cost.
> You didn't wonder whether a man's work was worth his wage. You
> wondered whether you could afford the wage.

At work, after he has seen a camp working captives:

> Chains don't have a rate. My grandmother's people were sold
> through Louisville before the Civil War - investment, the papers
> called them, and fed like it, and priced like it. I know exactly
> what that looks like from the other side of the ledger. Don't
> stand there and call it wages.

Under threat:

> Rope on his wrists at the bean rows. That's not work. Move.

## Seed 2 - the moral, the pacified, and the psychopath at one gate

Carriers: `everyone` for the moral one; the pacified one carries
what his environment let him keep; the psychopath carries nothing
that weighs. Three men at the same gate of the same camp, watching
the same arrangement - a captive hoeing the bean rows to feed the
gate. The wording differs, and the difference is the seed.

The moral one - morality still stands:

> That fella came up the road on foot with a rope on his wrists.
> Whatever they're calling it out here now, back in June we called
> it kidnapping, and it's still kidnapping with a bean row under it.

The pacified one - lulled and coerced, pacified by what he has
watched; flat, hedged, the arrangement speaking, no I:

> Gate eats every day. He hoes, it gets fed. That's the arrangement.
> Keep your head down and carry your part, and don't ask where the
> seed came from.

The psychopath - no clause that weighs anything:

> You want to know the trick of it? People are the cheapest stock
> left standing. They walk their own selves to the trough.

## Seed 3 - the dead's journey, not necessarily ended

Carriers: `county` for who Doreen was; the form's walking by
proximity and testimony - SAO's adaptation law, never a shared
encyclopedia.

Ordinary company:

> That's Doreen Prosser turning in at the ambulance bay. Third
> shift, before. She still walks her round like it's owing. I don't
> fire near the hospital - not because I think she's due back.
> Because some part of her is still clocked in, and that part gets
> to finish its shift.

Under threat:

> Doreen. Left door. Move.
