# The curation manifest

Every item records its source and its license before it enters, and
its curation note says what it is FOR. Curation opened 2026-09-13, the
day the nine world documents were approved (RECORD.md 43) and the
operator ruled what this corpus is for (RECORD.md 44): not only facts,
but the grounds a person's judgment stands on.

The first vein is United States Government work, which carries no
copyright (17 U.S.C. §105). The world documents already cite these
publications as fact; the corpus takes the same pages as language.

The consent question, settled per the README's rule: these are
official publications of the United States Government, written by
federal employees in the line of duty and published as the
government's own work without copyright. They are not the personal
speech of a living person, and no personal statement about a living
person's life enters with them.

## The entertainment-media vein, and its limit

The operator asked 2026-09-13 for archived media reviews and
transcripts, to train the period's cultural language and give people
varying degrees of memories of the world (RECORD.md 45). The vein has
a limit the corpus rules set: the archived reviews themselves -
Variety, the Billboard issues, the UPI wire copy - and the
commercial hearing/broadcast transcripts (C-SPAN, Federal News
Service) are copyrighted, and none of it can enter here. Where their
content lives instead: the facts of the period's entertainment are
carried in media.md as sourced claims, scoped per person by
who-knows-what; the era's official speech about entertainment enters
below, from the government vein; and the audiences' language -
how a person talks about what they followed, and how much of it they
kept - is authored as voice seeds (seeds 4 and 5), demonstrated per
carriers, never a shared encyclopedia.
